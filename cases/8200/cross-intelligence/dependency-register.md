# Dependency Register — Case 001

This register converts documented public evidence into numbered dependency records. It does not infer classified activity or person-level responsibility.

## D1-01 — NSA / ISNU information relationship

- **Class:** D1 Information
- **Documented period:** 2009
- **External actor:** U.S. National Security Agency (NSA)
- **Israeli node:** Israeli SIGINT National Unit (ISNU), identified in the released material with Unit 8200
- **Documentary basis:** released NSA–ISNU Memorandum of Understanding
- **Governance:** the MOU states that it remains in force until modified or rescinded by mutual agreement; either party may terminate it by written notice; terms are to be reviewed at least every two years; amendments are written and executed by authorized delegates.
- **Analytical significance:** this is not merely evidence of information exchange. It documents a formal governance architecture around the relationship.
- **Boundary:** this does not identify any individual officer as personally responsible for the agreement.

## D2-01 — Microsoft Azure computing/storage dependency

- **Class:** D2 Compute / D3 Storage
- **Documented period:** 2021–2022 onward
- **External actor:** Microsoft Azure
- **Evidence:** reporting describes a late-2021 meeting involving Microsoft's CEO and the then commander of Unit 8200, followed by engineering work and an Azure environment becoming operational in 2022.
- **Reason reported:** sources said the unit lacked sufficient internal storage/computing capacity for the intended workload.
- **Governance:** the reported architecture involved a segregated cloud environment and additional security controls.
- **Boundary:** reported facts and disputed claims must remain separated from independently verified technical facts.

## D4-01 — European physical infrastructure

- **Class:** D4 Infrastructure
- **Documented period:** 2022–2025
- **Jurisdiction:** Europe, including Netherlands and Ireland in reporting
- **Evidence:** reporting described Israeli military data held in Microsoft datacentres in Europe.
- **Analytical significance:** the capability therefore acquired an infrastructure layer governed partly outside the organisation's own physical environment.
- **Boundary:** reporting itself notes uncertainty about which Israeli military units' data accounted for all reported volumes.

## D6-01 — Provider governance / service interruption

- **Class:** D6 Governance
- **Documented period:** September 2025
- **External actor:** Microsoft
- **Event:** Microsoft announced that it had ceased and disabled a set of services to a unit within Israel's Ministry of Defense after an external review.
- **Analytical significance:** this demonstrates that an external technology provider can become a capability constraint without belonging to the military chain of command.
- **Boundary:** the exact scope of disabled services and the complete downstream technical consequences should not be inferred beyond the public record.

## D5-01 — Sovereignty / alternative-infrastructure question

- **Class:** D5 Sovereignty
- **Status:** research question, not established dependency
- **Question:** when sensitive capability depends on commercial cloud infrastructure outside the organisation's direct environment, what alternative sovereign or government-controlled infrastructure exists, and what documentary evidence shows whether it was selected, rejected, or used?
- **Rule:** absence of a public record is not evidence that an alternative did not exist.

## Cross-record pattern

**Information exchange → computing/storage → foreign physical infrastructure → provider governance**

This sequence is an analytical model derived from the numbered records. It is not a claim that every dependency was caused by the preceding one.

## Source discipline

The 2009 MOU is primary documentary evidence for its own terms. Later cloud events rely on public reporting and corporate statements and contain disputed elements. Those evidence classes must remain distinct.

## Core question

**Who can enable, constrain, or interrupt a capability without belonging to the organisation's formal chain of command?**
