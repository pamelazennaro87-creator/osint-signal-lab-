## Research target

Find the smallest public evidence set capable of connecting two or more fragments without relying solely on an intelligence-service attribution supplied by one source.


## First populated cluster: self-igniting parcel operation

### F-001 — Lithuania-origin parcel
- Four parcels were sent from Lithuania in July 2024 toward the UK and Poland.
- One ignited at Leipzig airport; another ignited in a UK warehouse.
- Authorities later identified additional test packages sent toward the United States and Canada.
- Source: Eurojust, 6 Mar 2026.

### F-002 — Poland parcel
- One parcel ignited in a truck in Poland; another was seized before ignition.
- The seized package allowed authorities to analyse the device and its potential effects.
- Source: Eurojust, 6 Mar 2026.

### F-003 — Amsterdam parcels
- Two parcels in Amsterdam were identified as intended for the United States and Canada and were suspected to be test packages.
- Source: Eurojust, 6 Mar 2026.

### Cross-fragment evidence

Eurojust states that prospective perpetrators were recruited and instructed through an online messaging service, that tasks were divided among several alleged perpetrators, and that investigators coordinated across Germany, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Poland and the United Kingdom to detect links to other arson investigations.

This is materially stronger than simply observing similar incidents: a joint investigation team explicitly worked to identify cross-case links.

### Current assessment

**F2 — linked**, not F4.

Reason: the public Eurojust record establishes a common investigation and divided tasks, but the public release does not expose enough person-to-fragment detail to reconstruct the complete operational graph.
