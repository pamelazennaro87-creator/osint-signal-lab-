# Case 004 — Source Register

## S1 — Lithuanian State Security Department

**Type:** Primary institutional source  
**Role:** Documents the service's public assessment of online recruitment through Telegram, including targeting of socially vulnerable people.

**Use:** Recruitment mechanism and vulnerability hypothesis.

**Caution:** This is an intelligence-service assessment from the authority responsible for Lithuanian national security. It is strong evidence of the agency's stated assessment, not independent proof of every underlying case.

## S2 — European Broadcasting Union / DW / LRT / RTVE / ČT investigation, September 2026

**Type:** Secondary investigative source with multi-country reporting  
**Role:** Reports a cross-border investigation into recruits linked by authorities to Russian-directed sabotage.

**Use:** Cross-border network reconstruction and source-genealogy work.

**Caution:** The investigation combines documents, interviews and classified material that is not itself fully public. Individual attribution claims require case-level corroboration.

## S3 — European reporting on online sabotage recruitment

**Type:** Secondary investigative reporting  
**Role:** Documents recruitment through online channels and informal employment environments.

**Use:** Recruitment-surface taxonomy and comparison with official assessments.

**Caution:** Do not count multiple publications repeating the same investigation as independent corroboration.

## S4 — Europol violence-as-a-service framework

**Type:** Primary institutional source  
**Role:** Provides a broader criminal-network comparison in which vulnerable young people are recruited and directed online for violence-for-hire.

**Use:** Comparative model only; it does not establish that every disposable-agent case belongs to organised crime rather than intelligence activity.

## Source genealogy rule

The number of URLs is not the number of independent sources.

For each claim, record:
- original investigator;
- original document;
- quoted authority;
- derivative reporting;
- independent corroboration;
- unresolved attribution.

## Evidence status

No claim in this register should be upgraded to high confidence solely because it appears in multiple outlets that derive from the same investigation.

## S6 — RUSI, “Responding to Russian Sabotage Financing”

**Type:** Specialist research / workshop-based assessment

RUSI describes a “gig-economy era” recruitment model in which low-cost assignments are outsourced to disposable individuals recruited online. It identifies encrypted messaging services and other digital platforms as recruitment environments and describes financially motivated one-day actors.

**Use:** Structural model of remote recruitment and the economics of disposable-agent activity.

**Caution:** RUSI's publication should not be converted into a claim that every job advertisement is Russian intelligence recruitment.

## S7 — September 2026 reporting on classified-ad platforms

A September 2026 Ukrainian investigation explicitly describes Russian intelligence recruitment through **anonymous Telegram channels and classified-ad platforms**, alongside social-media recruitment. It reports that young people and migrants are among the groups most frequently targeted.

**Evidence status:** Secondary reporting. The underlying RUSI material cited by the report should be treated separately from the reporter's reconstruction.

### Critical terminology correction

The portfolio should distinguish:

**job newspaper / printed classified → online classified-ad platform → job board → job-seeker platform → social-media group → messaging app**

The evidence currently establishes **classified-ad platforms / employment-oriented online channels**, but I have not yet found a sufficiently strong source proving that the present Russian sabotage networks specifically used a **printed newspaper employment advertisement**.

Therefore “newspaper” remains an open source-hunting target rather than a confirmed fact.

## S8 — Washington Post, 18 Aug 2023: direct employment-to-recruitment chain

Source: Greg Miller, Loveday Morris, Mary Ilyushina, The Washington Post, “Russia recruited operatives online to target weapons crossing Poland.”

URL: https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2023/08/18/ukraine-weapons-sabotage-gru-poland/

This is currently the closest match found to the original research hypothesis because the article documents a sequence beginning with apparently ordinary online job listings and progressing into intelligence/sabotage recruitment.

Reported sequence:
online job listing -> low-paid/menial task -> anonymous employer -> further assignments -> reconnaissance / intelligence activity

The listings appeared among employment-related and other community content used by refugee groups in Poland. The Washington Post reports that some respondents were refugees from eastern Ukraine seeking quick income.

Evidence boundary: this source establishes online employment-style recruitment, not a printed newspaper advertisement. It strongly supports the broader hypothesis of an employment surface as a recruitment vector, while leaving the printed-newspaper hypothesis unresolved.

Analytical significance: the critical transition is task escalation: the apparent employment relationship becomes a channel for progressively more sensitive assignments.

False-confirmation controls:
- job advertisement does not itself prove intelligence recruitment
- low-paid work does not itself prove deliberate targeting
- refugee/migrant status does not prove vulnerability or manipulation
- later espionage/sabotage assignment must be independently documented before connecting it to the initial employment contact
- one documented chain demonstrates a case pattern, not universal prevalence

## S9 — CyberDefence24, 21 Aug 2023: corroboration of the same Polish case
