# Case 004 — Disposable Agents / Online Recruitment

## Research question

How are low-level or one-time recruits approached online for criminal or sabotage activity, and what public evidence can establish the recruitment chain without conflating a recruit's actions with the identity of the remote controller?

## Why this case exists

Recent European reporting and official security assessments describe online recruitment of people for low-level tasks linked to Russian intelligence or other criminal networks.

The important OSINT problem is not simply attribution. It is reconstructing the chain:

`recruitment surface → recruiter/intermediary → recruit → assigned activity → payment/incentive → remote direction → attribution`

Each edge requires separate evidence.

## Current public evidence

The Lithuanian State Security Department states that Russian intelligence services have used Telegram groups containing propaganda, job advertisements and other illicit offers, targeting socially vulnerable people with monetary incentives. It warns that people responding to such offers may later be drawn into sabotage or other criminal activity.

European investigative reporting has separately documented one-time operatives recruited online for sabotage, including through informal employment channels.

In September 2026, an EBU investigation reported a cross-border network of recruits linked by authorities to Russian-directed sabotage activity in Czechia, Poland, Romania and Lithuania during 2024.

## Important distinction

The term **“disposable agent”** is useful as a reported description of a recruitment model, but it should not be treated as proof that every online recruit is an intelligence agent.

For every case we separate:

1. what the recruit was offered;
2. what the recruit actually did;
3. what investigators attribute to a recruiter;
4. what investigators attribute to a state service;
5. what remains unresolved.

## Vulnerability hypothesis

A specific sub-question is whether economic hardship, unstable employment, marginalisation, homelessness or other vulnerabilities are systematically exploited.

This is **unresolved at case opening**.

We will not turn the hypothesis into a fact merely because it is plausible.

## Alternative hypotheses

- **H1 — State-directed recruitment:** a state intelligence service deliberately recruits disposable local operatives.
- **H2 — Criminal intermediary:** recruiters operate primarily as criminal entrepreneurs, with state direction established only in some cases.
- **H3 — Mixed ecosystem:** state actors, criminal intermediaries and ideologically motivated recruits coexist.
- **H4 — Attribution overreach:** some incidents publicly grouped under one campaign may have different origins.

## False-confirmation tests

Telegram contact ≠ intelligence recruitment.

Russian-language content ≠ Russian state control.

Payment in cryptocurrency ≠ state sponsorship.

A recruit's nationality ≠ the nationality of the controller.

An alleged sabotage act ≠ proof of the full command chain.

A media description of a network ≠ independent confirmation of every incident attributed to it.

## Safety boundary

This case studies recruitment and attribution using public evidence. It does not reproduce operational instructions for sabotage, identify vulnerable individuals, or provide actionable instructions for carrying out attacks.
