# Case 003 — Digital Trafficking

## Research question

How do online recruitment systems transform economic or social vulnerability into human-trafficking risk, and where does the public evidence chain become incomplete?

## Scope

This case examines publicly documented recruitment mechanisms rather than individual victims.

Focus areas:
- false employment advertisements;
- fake employment agencies and websites;
- passive recruitment of people already seeking work;
- online grooming and profiling;
- digital control;
- cross-border recruitment and exploitation;
- differences between recorded cases and likely prevalence.

## Evidence boundary

The case does not identify victims, publish personal data, or attempt to contact suspected recruiters.

The analytical unit is the **recruitment mechanism**, not the individual.

## Initial evidence

Europol documents two broad online recruitment patterns: active recruitment through false job advertisements and fake employment agencies, and passive recruitment in which criminals approach people who publicly advertise that they are looking for work abroad.

Europol also describes the use of online information to profile vulnerabilities and the use of digital tools during later stages of control.

## Investigation chain

`recruitment surface → contact → deception → movement/control → exploitation → detection → registration`

The chain is intentionally incomplete. A central objective is to identify where public datasets lose information.

## Core hypotheses

- **H1 — Platform-mediated recruitment:** online employment/social platforms materially expand the recruitment surface.
- **H2 — Vulnerability targeting:** recruiters select or exploit observable economic/social vulnerability.
- **H3 — Evidence loss:** official statistics capture only a subset of the underlying recruitment-to-exploitation chain.
- **H4 — Jurisdictional distortion:** differences in definitions, detection and recording practices make cross-country comparisons difficult.

These are research hypotheses, not conclusions.

## False-confirmation tests

A job advertisement alone does not establish trafficking.

A suspicious website alone does not establish a criminal network.

A recorded trafficking case does not establish how the victim was first recruited unless the source documents that link.

A rise in recorded cases does not automatically mean a rise in underlying prevalence.

## Planned outputs

- cross-jurisdiction source matrix;
- recruitment-pattern taxonomy;
- evidence-gap map;
- alternative-hypothesis register;
- reproducible source register;
- final case assessment with confidence levels.

## Primary standard

> The conclusion must never be stronger than the evidence supporting it.
