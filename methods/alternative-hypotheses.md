# Alternative Hypotheses & Divergence

## Purpose

Every significant reconstruction should survive an attempt to break it.

## Required branches

### H1 — Preferred reconstruction
The explanation best supported by the current evidence.

### H2 — Competing reconstruction
A materially different explanation that fits a meaningful part of the evidence.

### H3 — Source-artifact explanation
The apparent pattern may result from copied reporting, stale data, naming conventions or database errors.

### H4 — Missing-data explanation
The public record may be incomplete enough that neither H1 nor H2 can be established.

## Red-team questions

- What would have to be true for this conclusion to be wrong?
- Which source is doing most of the work?
- Is that source independent?
- Could two entities share the same identifier?
- Could the chronology be misleading?
- Is a role being mistaken for a person?
- Is an employee being mistaken for a company?
- Is intended capability being mistaken for deployed capability?
- Is absence of evidence being treated as evidence of absence?

## Divergence rule

A competing hypothesis is not a failure of the investigation.

If it survives testing, it belongs in the result.

> **The goal is not to protect the hypothesis. The goal is to map the evidence space.**
