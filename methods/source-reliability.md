# Source Reliability & Independence

## Purpose

This method prevents repeated reporting from being mistaken for independent corroboration.

## Source classes

### PRIMARY
First-party or original material:

- government records;
- regulatory/sanctions lists;
- official announcements;
- original datasets;
- original presentations;
- direct public statements;
- contemporaneous records.

### SECONDARY
Reporting or analysis that describes primary material:

- investigative journalism;
- specialist reporting;
- academic analysis;
- industry databases.

### TERTIARY
Republished or derivative material:

- aggregators;
- copied articles;
- summaries;
- pages reproducing another outlet's reporting.

Tertiary material can help discovery but should rarely close an evidentiary gap.

## Independence test

For every apparent corroboration ask:

1. Did the source conduct its own reporting?
2. Does it cite the same underlying document?
3. Does it quote the same person?
4. Did it publish independently or reproduce another article?
5. Does it add a genuinely new fact?

Two articles can therefore equal **one evidentiary lineage**.

## Reliability is claim-specific

A source is not simply "reliable" or "unreliable".

Assess:

- authority for this specific claim;
- proximity to the underlying event;
- transparency of sourcing;
- date;
- potential incentives;
- corroboration;
- contradictions.

## Rule

> **Independence is a property of the evidence chain, not the number of URLs.**
